Exercise 1: Riemann and Lebesgue Integration

The fundamental difference between Riemann and Lebesgue integration lies in how the domain/range space is partitioned:

  • Riemann Approach (Domain Partitioning): Subdivides the domain interval $[a, b]$ into subintervals $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$. The integral is approximated as $R_n = \sum_{i=1}^n f(\xi_i) \Delta x_i$.
  • Lebesgue Approach (Range Partitioning): Subdivides the range (y-values) into slices $[y_{j-1}, y_j]$ and computes the measure $\mu(E_j)$ of the preimage sets $E_j = \{x \in [a, b] : y_{j-1} \le f(x) < y_j\}$. The integral is $L_m = \sum_{j=1}^m y_j^* \, \mu(E_j)$.

Pathological Functions Comparison

Consider the Dirichlet Function $f(x) = \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}(x)$ on $[0, 1]$:

  • Riemann: In every subinterval $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$, there are both rationals ($f=1$) and irrationals ($f=0$). The upper Darboux sum is $1$ and lower sum is $0$. Thus, $f(x)$ is not Riemann integrable.
  • Lebesgue: The preimage of $\{1\}$ is $\mathbb{Q} \cap [0, 1]$ (measure 0), and the preimage of $\{0\}$ is $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q} \cap [0, 1]$ (measure 1). The Lebesgue integral evaluates strictly to $1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 1 = 0$.

Exercise 2: Numerical Computation of the Normal CDF

The Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of the Standard Normal Distribution is given by: $$\Phi(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^x e^{-\frac{t^2}{2}} \, dt$$

1. Liouville's Theorem & Non-Elementarity

By Liouville's Theorem (and the Risch algorithm in differential algebra), an elementary function has an elementary antiderivative if and only if it can be expressed in a specific algebraic form. The integrand $e^{-t^2/2}$ lacks such an antiderivative in terms of elementary functions (polynomials, exponentials, logarithms, trigonometric functions). Hence, $\Phi(x)$ cannot be written in closed elementary form.

2. Analytical Representation via Error Function ($\text{erf}$)

By introducing the special Error Function $\text{erf}(z) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \int_0^z e^{-u^2} du$, we obtain the exact identity: $$\Phi(x) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 + \text{erf}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right) \right]$$

3. Numerical Implementation Approaches

  • Simpson's Rule: Numerical integration over $[-10, x]$ dividing the interval into $N$ steps.
  • Maclaurin Series Expansion: $\text{erf}(z) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k z^{2k+1}}{k! (2k+1)}$. Effective for small $|z|$.
  • Abramowitz & Stegun Approximation: High-precision rational approximation formula (Accuracy $\sim 1.5 \times 10^{-7}$).
