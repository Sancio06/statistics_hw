1. Stochastic Penetration Process Models

Consider $n$ sequential security systems (or steps) attacked by $m$ independent attackers. The penetration process is modeled as a sum of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$:

  • Bernoulli Model ($X_i \in \{0, 1\}$): Represents binary outcome attacks (Failure = 0, Success = 1) with $P(X_i = 1) = p$. Expectation $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = p$ and variance $\text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p)$.
  • Rademacher Model ($X_i \in \{-1, +1\}$): Represents symmetric zero-mean random walks with $P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = 0.5$. Expectation $\mathbb{E}[X_i] = 0$ and variance $\text{Var}(X_i) = 1$.

Three Trajectory Quantities

Total Score $S_n$

$$S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$$ Variance grows linearly: $\text{Var}(S_n) = n \sigma^2$. Trajectories diverge as $n \to \infty$.

Average Score $S_n / n$

$$\frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$$ Variance shrinks: $\text{Var}\left(\frac{S_n}{n}\right) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \to 0$. Converges to $\mu$ by LLN.

Normalized Score $S_n / \sqrt{n}$

$$\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$$ Variance remains constant: $\text{Var}\left(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = \sigma^2$. Converges to Normal distribution by CLT.

3. Mathematical Analysis: LLN vs. CLT

Convergence in Probability (Law of Large Numbers)

By the Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN), for any $\varepsilon > 0$: $$\lim_{n \to \infty} P\left(\left| \frac{S_n}{n} - \mu \right| \ge \varepsilon\right) = 0$$ In the simulation, as $n$ increases, the trajectories for $S_n/n$ collapse onto a flat line corresponding to the expected mean ($\mu = 0$ for Rademacher, $\mu = 0.5$ for Bernoulli). The variance decays as $\mathcal{O}(1/n)$.

Convergence in Distribution (Central Limit Theorem)

By the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), for independent Rademacher variables ($\mu = 0, \sigma^2 = 1$), the scaled sum satisfies: $$Z_n = \frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} = \frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$ When normalizing by $\sqrt{n}$, the variance is stabilized at $\text{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) = 1$. The empirical distribution of terminal scores across $m$ attackers converges to the bell curve of a Standard Normal distribution.

4. Cybersecurity Context & Interpretation

  • Effect of System Size ($n$): In large complex infrastructures ($n \to \infty$), aggregate metrics like $S_n/n$ become highly predictable due to statistical averaging. However, raw total score $S_n$ fluctuates severely with standard deviation scaling as $\sqrt{n}$.
  • Effect of Number of Attackers ($m$): As the number of simultaneous attackers increases, the ensemble average approaches theoretical distributions (Gaussian under CLT), illustrating how threat actor behavior stabilizes at scale.
