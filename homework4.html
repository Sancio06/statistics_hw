Exercise 1: Derivation of Welford's Recurrence Formulas

In online data streams, computing sample variance using the standard formula $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - \frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right)^2$ leads to catastrophic cancellation due to floating-point precision loss. B.P. Welford (1962) introduced an exact single-pass algorithm updating mean $\bar{x}_n$ and sum of squared differences $M_{2,n} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x}_n)^2$.

1. Knuth's Mean Recurrence

$$\bar{x}_n = \frac{(n-1)\bar{x}_{n-1} + x_n}{n} = \bar{x}_{n-1} + \frac{x_n - \bar{x}_{n-1}}{n}$$

2. Welford's Sum of Squares ($M_{2,n}$) Recurrence

Split the sum into the first $(n-1)$ observations and the $n$-th observation: $$M_{2,n} = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_n)^2 + (x_n - \bar{x}_n)^2$$ Substituting $x_i - \bar{x}_n = (x_i - \bar{x}_{n-1}) - \frac{x_n - \bar{x}_{n-1}}{n}$ and simplifying yields the core update: $$M_{2,n} = M_{2,n-1} + (x_n - \bar{x}_{n-1})(x_n - \bar{x}_n)$$

3. Core Implementation (3 Lines)

let delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / count;
M2 += delta * (x - mean); // Uses updated mean

Exercise 2: Multi-Hacker Penetration Simulation & Inferential Analysis

Each penetration attempt yields a score $X_i \in [0, 10]$ drawn from a Discrete Uniform distribution over $\{0, 1, \dots, 10\}$.

  • Theoretical Mean: $\mu = \frac{0 + 10}{2} = 5.0000$
  • Theoretical Variance: $\sigma^2 = \frac{(10 - 0 + 1)^2 - 1}{12} = \frac{120}{12} = 10.0000$

Statistical Interpretation & Conclusions

  • Unbiasedness of Estimators: Across $m$ hackers, the grand average of sample means $\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m \bar{x}_j$ converges to the theoretical mean $\mu = 5.0$, and the grand average of sample variances $\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m s_j^2$ converges to $\sigma^2 = 10.0$. This demonstrates that sample mean and sample variance (with $n-1$ denominator) are unbiased estimators.
  • Effect of System Size ($n$): As $n$ increases, the variance of sample means shrinks proportionally to $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$. Individual hacker mean trajectories collapse tightly around $\mu = 5.0$.
  • Usefulness in Inferential Statistics: Welford's algorithm allows security operations centers (SOC) to process continuous, unbounded log streams in real time without storing historical scores, maintaining numerically stable statistical confidence intervals.
